# The Six Key Components - EOS's Model of a Whole Business

> **Up:** [[Home|Home]] → [[04-Frameworks-and-Mental-Models/Frameworks and Mental Models Index|Frameworks and Mental Models]] → [[04-Frameworks-and-Mental-Models/Focus-Execution-and-Systems/Focus Execution and Systems Map|Focus, Execution, and Systems]]

## One-line summary
Every organisation runs on six components — Vision, People, Data, Issues, Process, and Traction — and the founder's hundred worries collapse into a short diagnostic once you accept that any specific frustration is a symptom of one of the six being weak.

## Context
*Traction* is the manual for EOS, the **Entrepreneurial Operating System** — a complete operating system for a small-to-mid-sized company, in the sense that an OS is a set of interlocking tools everyone uses rather than a philosophy. Its central claim is compression. In one client's words: *"I used to worry about 100 different things. Once I learned there were six components to my business and I focused on only those, those 100 different things I'd been worrying about went away."*

**Naming note:** this is Gino Wickman's *Traction*, not Weinberg and Mares' [[04-Frameworks-and-Mental-Models/Strategy-and-Competitive-Advantage/Bullseye - Test All Channels, Then Focus on One|book of the same title]] about customer-acquisition channels. The two are unrelated.

## The six components

**1. Vision.** Everyone seeing the same picture of where the business is going and how it gets there. Wickman's test is brutal and cheap: ask each employee separately what the vision is and count how many different answers you get. The tool is the **V/TO**, which answers eight questions on two pages. The analogy is Al Ries's: the sun delivers billions of kilowatts and gives you sunburn; a few watts focused in one direction cuts diamonds.

**2. People.** *Right people in the right seats.* Two separate tests, deliberately kept separate — **right people** share your core values (tested with the People Analyzer); **right seats** means each person is operating in their Unique Ability inside a structure that actually makes sense (tested with the Accountability Chart and GWC).

**3. Data.** A **Scorecard** of five to fifteen high-level numbers reviewed weekly, plus one meaningful number owned by every person in the company. The point is escaping *"managing personalities, egos, subjective issues, emotions, and intangibles."* A P&L is a trailing indicator; by the time it tells you the score, the quarter is spent. A weekly activity-based scorecard lets you change the future.

**4. Issues.** Strengthening the first three components produces transparency — *an open organization where there is nowhere to hide* — which surfaces the problems that were already blocking you. Two tools: an **Issues List** at every level so problems are captured and prioritised rather than discussed ad hoc, and the **Issues Solving Track (IDS)** to close them permanently. Wickman's arithmetic: taking the time to solve a problem properly saves **two to ten times** that time later.

**5. Process.** *Your Way of doing business* — the core processes documented, taught, and followed consistently. He calls this the most neglected of the six, because most entrepreneurs keep the processes in their heads and improvise. You cannot scale what only exists in one person's memory.

**6. Traction.** Execution — *"vision without traction is merely hallucination."* Typically the weakest component; new clients self-rate accountability at about **4 out of 10**. Two disciplines only: **Rocks** (three to seven 90-day priorities per person) and a **Meeting Pulse** (a standing weekly and quarterly rhythm).

## How to use it as a diagnostic

The model's practical value is that it converts vague frustration into a located fault:

| Symptom | Weak component |
| --- | --- |
| People pulling in different directions; "why are we doing this?" | Vision |
| Chronic drama, a tolerated underperformer, unclear who owns what | People |
| Surprises that only appear in the monthly financials | Data |
| The same argument every month; nothing ever gets resolved | Issues |
| Quality varies by who does the work; can't onboard or delegate | Process |
| Good plans, nothing shipped; accountability rated ~4 | Traction |

Wickman's **Organizational Checkup** scores twenty statements 1–5. Most organisations sit **below 50%**; **above 80%** is the target, and reaching 100% in every component is explicitly not expected. The cadence is one reassessment every 90 days, with the goal of moving the number — not of jumping from 20% to 80% overnight.

## Why it is a system and not a list

The components reinforce each other in one direction. Vision without People produces plans nobody can execute. People without Data means you're managing on opinion. Data without Issues means you can see the problem and never fix it. Issues without Process means you solve the same one repeatedly. And all five without Traction is the consultant's two-day offsite that leaves everyone warm and changes nothing — Wickman's specific complaint about strategic planning that never comes down to the ground.

## Application

- Run the Organizational Checkup with your leadership team, then pick **the single lowest component** and work only on it for a quarter. The model's whole value is refusing to work on six things at once.
- Before adding a new initiative, ask which of the six it strengthens. If the answer isn't one of them, it is probably a distraction wearing the clothes of an opportunity.
- Use the symptom table above when a frustration recurs. Recurring frustration is a component fault, not a people fault — the exception being when the fault genuinely *is* the People component, which the Accountability Chart and GWC will tell you.
- Re-score every 90 days and keep the history. The trend is the signal; a single score is noise.
- If you already run OKRs, don't stack EOS on top. Read the six components as a diagnostic map and adopt the specific tools that fill your gaps — most commonly Data and Issues.
