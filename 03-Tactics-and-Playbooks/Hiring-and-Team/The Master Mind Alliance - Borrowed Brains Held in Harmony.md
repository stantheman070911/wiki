# The Master Mind Alliance - Borrowed Brains Held in Harmony

> **Up:** [[Home|Home]] → [[03-Tactics-and-Playbooks/Tactics and Playbooks Index|Tactics and Playbooks]]

## One-line summary
No individual has enough experience, education, or native ability to build anything large alone — so assemble a small standing group around one definite purpose, pay them in something they want, meet on a fixed cadence, and treat any breakdown in harmony as fatal rather than annoying.

## Context
Hill defines the Master Mind as "coordination of knowledge and effort, in a spirit of harmony, between two or more people, for the attainment of a definite purpose," and calls it the principle without which great power cannot be accumulated. Carnegie attributed his entire fortune to a group of roughly fifty men assembled for the definite purpose of manufacturing and marketing steel — and it was Carnegie who described the principle to Hill in the first place. Hill also offers a psychic explanation (two minds meeting create an invisible "third mind"); the operating instructions stand without it.

## The four setup rules

1. **Ally yourself with as many people as the plan actually needs.** Hill marks this as absolutely essential, not optional — his objection to solo planning is that your own experience, education, ability, and imagination are a fixed and insufficient stock.
2. **Decide in advance what you are offering each member** in return for their cooperation — before you approach anyone. No one works indefinitely without compensation, and it need not be money. Assuming otherwise is the most common way these groups die.
3. **Meet at least twice a week**, more if possible, until the plan is jointly perfected. A group that meets when convenient is not a Master Mind.
4. **Maintain perfect harmony.** Hill states flatly that the principle cannot operate where harmony does not prevail, and that failure on this point alone predicts failure of the whole enterprise.

Two further selection rules he adds elsewhere: choose people **who do not take defeat seriously** (you will need them when the plan fails), and choose people who are **in complete sympathy with your purpose** — because these are the only people you should take into your confidence at all.

## What the group is actually for

- **Plans should be the joint creation of the group, not yours alone.** You may originate them in whole or in part, but they must be checked and approved by the other members before you commit.
- **It supplies specialized knowledge you don't have and don't need to acquire.** See [[03-Tactics-and-Playbooks/Wealth-and-Skill-Building/Specialized Knowledge - Power Is Knowledge Organized and Directed|Specialized Knowledge]].
- **It is where persistence gets rebuilt.** Hill's fourth step for developing persistence is a friendly alliance with people who will encourage you to follow through, and he advises selecting at least one member for this specifically.
- **Association transfers habits.** Hill's read on Ford: his fastest gains came after his friendships with Edison, Firestone, Burroughs, and Burbank — "men take on the nature and the habits and the power of thought of those with whom they associate in a spirit of sympathy and harmony."

## The three-person round table, and the invisible counsellors

Hill describes the smallest practical form: he and two staff members sat at a table, stated the problem plainly, and each contributed whatever occurred to them — a procedure he found placed each participant "in communication with sources of knowledge definitely outside his own experience." Stripped of the mysticism, this is a structured three-way problem-solving session with a stated question, and Hill considered it the simplest usable application of the whole principle.

He also ran a solo variant for years: a nightly imagined council with nine figures he admired (Emerson, Paine, Edison, Darwin, Lincoln, Burbank, Napoleon, Ford, Carnegie), chairing the meeting and addressing each in turn for the quality he wanted from them. He is explicit that this was a character-building exercise via auto-suggestion, not a séance. It is a usable substitute when you have no group yet.

## Application

1. Write the definite purpose the group exists for, in one sentence. A Master Mind without a shared object is a networking circle.
2. List the capabilities the purpose requires and which you personally lack. Recruit against that list, not against who is available.
3. For each person, write down what they get — money, equity, access, learning, reciprocity, status — before you ask.
4. Fix the cadence and hold it. Weekly or twice weekly; a standing slot beats an agenda.
5. Treat friction as a five-alarm problem. Address it immediately or remove the member; a group in conflict produces worse plans than you would alone.
6. Bring every significant plan to the group for review before committing to it — and keep your plans out of every other conversation.
