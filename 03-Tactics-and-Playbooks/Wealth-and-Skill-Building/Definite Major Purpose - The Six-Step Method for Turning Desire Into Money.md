# Definite Major Purpose - The Six-Step Method for Turning Desire Into Money

> **Up:** [[Home|Home]] → [[03-Tactics-and-Playbooks/Tactics and Playbooks Index|Tactics and Playbooks]]

## One-line summary
Vague wanting produces nothing; write down an exact amount, an exact date, and an exact service you will render in exchange — then rehearse it aloud twice a day until the mind stops treating it as a wish and starts producing plans.

## Context
Hill's first and most-copied procedure, attributed to Andrew Carnegie and vetted by Thomas Edison. It exists because most people cannot answer "what do you want?" with a number, a date, and a plan — Hill claimed 98 of 100 people he interviewed could not. The method's real function is not mystical: it forces a fuzzy aspiration through four filters (amount, price, deadline, plan) that a wish cannot survive, and then keeps it top-of-mind long enough to change what you notice and act on.

## The six steps

1. **Fix the exact amount.** Not "plenty of money" — a specific figure. Definiteness is the whole point; the mind cannot aim at a range.
2. **Determine exactly what you will give in return.** There is no something-for-nothing. Name the service, product, or skill you will exchange for it.
3. **Set a definite date** by which you intend to possess it.
4. **Create a definite plan and begin at once — ready or not.** Starting before the plan is good is deliberate: the plan will be revised, the habit of acting will not form otherwise.
5. **Write it as one clear, concise statement** containing all four elements — amount, deadline, service rendered, plan.
6. **Read the statement aloud twice daily**, on waking and before sleep, seeing and *feeling* yourself already in possession.

Hill's own worked template: *"By [date] I will have in my possession $X, which will come to me in various amounts from time to time. In return I will give the fullest possible quantity and the best possible quality of service as [role/offer]. I believe I will have this money in my possession … I am awaiting a plan by which to accumulate it, and I will follow that plan when it is received."*

## Why the reading-aloud step is not optional

Hill's mechanism is auto-suggestion: repetition without emotion does nothing. He is explicit that reciting Coué's "day by day, in every way…" a million times unemotionally produces no result, because only feeling-charged thoughts get acted on. The practical translation, stripped of his 1937 metaphysics: the statement has to be rehearsed until it carries conviction, not until it is memorised. Two supporting mechanics:

- **Visualise rendering the service, not just receiving the money.** Hill insists you picture yourself delivering the goods. This keeps the exercise from degenerating into fantasy about outcomes.
- **The self-confidence formula.** A signed, memorised, daily-recited pledge covering five commitments: persistent action toward the purpose; thirty minutes daily picturing the person you intend to become; ten minutes daily on self-confidence; a written Definite Chief Aim you will not abandon; and a refusal to engage in any transaction that does not benefit everyone it affects.

## Burning the ships

Definiteness alone is weak; Hill's examples all involve destroying the retreat. Edwin Barnes rode a freight train to Edison's laboratory and announced he had come to go into *business with* Edison, not work for him — then spent five years in menial work without a single encouraging sign, refusing to hold a backup plan. After the Chicago fire, one merchant chose to rebuild on the same spot while every peer relocated. Hill's rule: a desire that keeps a line of retreat open never becomes an obsession, and only an obsession survives the years of no visible progress.

## Application

- Convert your current goal into the six-part statement today. If you cannot name the figure, the date, or what you'll give in return, you do not have a goal — you have a mood.
- Put the written statement where you see it on waking and before sleep. Read it aloud; silent reading is not the exercise.
- Test for retreat lines. Write down the sentence that begins "if this doesn't work I'll just…" — that sentence is what will actually happen unless you close it off.
- Re-derive the plan freely; keep the purpose fixed. Step 4 says start with an imperfect plan, and [[04-Frameworks-and-Mental-Models/Focus-Execution-and-Systems/Temporary Defeat Is a Plan Signal - Organized Planning and the Quitter's Test|Temporary Defeat Is a Plan Signal]] covers what to do when it fails.
- Review annually against Hill's self-analysis questionnaire (did I hit the year's objective? was my service the best quality and quantity I was capable of? where did procrastination cost me?).
